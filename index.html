Preface

There are years that leave a mark — not loudly, not all at once, but in the slow accumulation of ordinary midnights, unanswered questions, and songs that somehow say the thing you couldn't.

This is one of those years. Written down.

These pages weren't planned. They arrived the way most honest things do — uninvited, between other obligations, with a kind of urgency that felt embarrassing until it didn't. Words are strange like that. They wait. And when you're not looking, they insist.

What you'll find here isn't resolution. It's the shape of a particular confusion — the kind that comes from wanting too much, feeling too quietly, and standing at the edge of something you can't yet name. Seven movements. One long breath.

Perhaps you know this place. The hours that are neither late nor early. The feeling of becoming something without knowing what. The moon, still there, still bright, embarrassingly faithful.

Read carefully, or carelessly. But if a line stops you — stay with it a moment. That's where this was written from.

— P

The Speed of Things
on the world moving faster than you agreed to

Pascal once wrote that all of humanity's problems stem from the inability to sit quietly in a room alone. He meant it as a warning. In practice it reads as permission — to slow down, to feel the lag, to let the world outpace you without calling it failure. The headphones in this poem are not avoidance. They are cartography. A way of locating yourself when the map keeps changing.

on overwhelm — blaise pascal · pensées
Life's moving fast, I feel my heart stuck in slow-mo. All the things that once made sense — now I'm like… wait, was that a no-go? Things that felt simple and fun now chase me like I owe them money in this run. A boy finds peace in songs, sings along just to feel he belongs. Headphones in, world on mute, low battery — but still kind of cute. Reality hits. No warning. No crown. Just shows up one day and sits you down. The leaves have slowly started to brown, even the sky feels a little less town. Life moves on, hands out wings, says "fly high." But sometimes it's fine to just sit… and cry. And somehow, between the chaos and the plea, life starts making a little sense to me — not perfect, not whole, not fully free… but enough to finally just… be.
the opening

A collection needs a before — a moment before anything is understood. This is that moment. Not dramatic. Not crisis. Just the quiet vertigo of realizing the world has been rearranged while you were sleeping. The poem earns its tenderness by refusing to overdramatize. The disorientation is real; the humor is realer. Both coexist. That's the tone for everything that follows.

on one line

"Low battery, but still kind of cute" is probably the most honest sentence in the collection. It holds, in eight words, the central posture of the whole book: aware of its own depletion, unwilling to be destroyed by it. Every other poem is a variation on that line.
